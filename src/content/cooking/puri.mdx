I had given this quite many tries before, end to end tries always failed with a flat taco (Puri is soft and the more spherical it comes out, more the envy/awe among spectators).

One thing that is learned about the fried things is you knead it will a bit of oil once till you can form a dough ball which can hold itself.
This gives softer and fluffier output.
From my latest information (with 50 percent accuracy) from the most esteemed sources (instagra reels), what I could understand is that the oil helps to not let the protein in flour to form hard bonds, it restricts hard bonding when water comes in.
Once the dough is kneaded with oil, adding the right amount of water so that the dough is not hard without moisture but also not too much moisture (just enough to be sticky to a bit hands).
Unlinke other breads which I have worked on, for this one it is not recommended to roll the dough with dry flour (the dry floor with ruin the oil with small residues rendering the oil useless for future use).
The dough is rolled with oil so that it doesn't stick.

Now comes the oil work. My experience with breads worked here. One of the secrets to makking a fluffy bread is to tranfer the air once it starts forming (this is done by pressing where the dough rises and directing the air to where you would want it to go).
To visualize, it would look like submerging something repeatedly as it comes up to the surface placing your spatula where the air starts growing.
