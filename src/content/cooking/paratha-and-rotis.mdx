Contrary to the prevalent thought that rolling a roti or paratha in a perfect circle is difficult, I have found "getting the correct dough consistency" to be the toughest.

I realized early that rolling the dough in a circle required the dough to somehow spin while being rolled. With close observation I found that needs hands to be left loose and pressure applied in a (somewhat) rotatory motion (not something that you can catch by observation but comes with idea).

One of the things that I learned very late (due to ignorance), that dough should be soft for best results (I have been a hard dough maker for it's ease in handling). The softer the dough, the more filling it can hold on being stuffed.

  layout="masonry"

Leaving the dough to rest for some time also improved the quality (I don't know why exactly, maybe the moisture spreads uniformly throughout the dough giving it a uniform consistency).

Controlling heat also matters. Making paratha on continuous slow flame will make the paratha/roti hard. Good way is high-medium flame initially and then lowering heat at the end (after paratha is made and you are preparing the next one to go in)

Cool trick to make paratha less oily is to put raw rolled paratha on the completed paratha. The raw one will soak the oil.

Also when struggling initially, starting small helps a lot. Make small roti and paratha. make paratha less oily is to put raw rolled paratha on the completed paratha. The raw one will soak the oil.

# My goto and relief food it is

With dough ready, making paratha is very easy for me now. It is also very quick to make and people like to have it. The filling for some variations are very easy to prepare (paneer, sattu, soyabean (very good) etc).

# Still a long way

I still need to learn how I can stuff in more stuffing. Eating at a stall during my visit to Gurgaon once and having paratha there I realized I am not even close to making the very soft and flowly dough. They stuffed an insane amount of stuffing because of the dough. Their technique (roll the dough once and then add stuffing) was also new. A lot to learn still.
